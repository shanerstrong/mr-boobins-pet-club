# Adult Jack likeness specification — current photo set

Date: 2026-08-16

Reference status: ten current photographs supplied by Mark, viewed locally and
not copied into the repository or Drive mirror. Photos 1–4 establish the
standing silhouette; Photos 5–6 and 10 establish the back, pelvis, and tail;
Photos 7–9 establish the face and relaxed down pose. Perspective and motion
blur prevent photogrammetric precision, so the ranges below are comparison
targets rather than medical measurements.

## Preserve

- Short warm-white coat with subtle cream shading, never gray plastic.
- Upright triangular ears with visible pink interiors and a slightly relaxed,
  non-mechanical asymmetry.
- Long continuous shepherd wedge from forehead through muzzle.
- Broad mauve-charcoal nose with organic nostrils and a soft central groove.
- Dark almond eyes set into the face rather than pasted-on round discs.
- Friendly side-opening smile with restrained teeth and tongue rooted inside
  the mouth.
- Blue collar; a simple small metal tag may remain.

## Correct adult silhouette

- Long, lean trunk with a nearly level back: shoulder-to-croup length should
  read roughly 0.75–0.88 of standing shoulder height in the fixed side view.
- Deep chest behind the forelegs, followed by a visible abdominal tuck; waist
  depth should read about 20–30% smaller than chest depth.
- Tall slim forelegs with elbows and wrists visible; remove the current
  column-like legs and inflated puppy paws.
- Rear assembly must show thigh, hock, and planted rear paw as one continuous
  canine chain. Jack's hindquarters are substantial but not spherical.
- Adult paws are sturdy but only modestly wider than the lower leg, not the
  oversized rounded Baby blocks.
- Tail begins low at the pelvis, reaches approximately to the hock or slightly
  below when relaxed, and tapers gradually with visible coat volume.
- Neck is long enough to transition cleanly into the shoulder and chest; avoid
  both the V2.2 stump-neck and the Quaternius tube-neck.

## Head targets

- Muzzle projection should occupy about 42–48% of total nose-to-occiput head
  length in a neutral side view.
- Nose width should read roughly 36–43% of the visible head width in a neutral
  front view.
- Eyes sit slightly oblique and well behind the nose bridge; reduce the generic
  round-eyed puppy expression.
- Ear bases must emerge from the skull, not from a flat top plane. Ear height
  should remain prominent without making the head read like a rabbit.
- Preserve the characteristic soft cheek and open smile without adding round
  cheek balls, front-facing fangs, or a floating tongue.

## Cheap checkpoint acceptance

Before rig work, render neutral front, side, and three-quarter views with the
same camera and lighting. The candidate remains exploratory until Mark decides
that it reads more like adult Jack. Technical success alone cannot approve the
look.

Reject or stop if:

- the new silhouette remains a stretched puppy;
- the tail, muzzle, or legs require destructive topology replacement;
- the candidate loses the existing UV/material or shape-key data;
- correction requires another paid generation;
- a later bind test produces folding, detached feet, or collapsed joints.
